Warhammer 40,000: Dawn of War - Dark Crusade is the second expansion pack to the PC-based real-time strategy (RTS) game Warhammer 40,000: Dawn of War developed by Relic Entertainment and published by THQ. Based on Games Workshop's popular tabletop wargame, Warhammer 40,000, Dark Crusade was released on October 9, 2006. The expansion features two new races, the T'au Empire and the Necrons. Including the Imperial Guard from Dawn of War's first expansion pack Winter Assault, this means a total of seven playable races in this expansion.

Unlike Winter Assault, Dark Crusade is a standalone expansion that does not require prior installation of Dawn of War or Winter Assault to play, allowing the user to play as all seven factions in both single player Skirmish and Campaign modes, though not in multiplayer.

Parallel to the release of Dark Crusade, THQ also released a triple pack of Dawn of War, Winter Assault, and Dark Crusade, dubbed Dawn of War Anthology. The case is embossed with images of all the faction leaders of the campaign dressed in their respective wargear.

Gameplay

As with previous Dawn of War titles, Dark Crusade is focused on the conflict portion of gameplay; in order to obtain more resources players must fight for them. Each player starts off with a base and wins by fulfilling mission objectives. There are multiple tiers of technology, with each allowing for more powerful units and upgrades.

Units

The number of units a player may field at one time is determined by population and vehicle 'squad caps'; these limit the number of infantry troops and vehicles a player may have on the battlefield. Squad caps may be increased using methods differing between races. Most units have a melee attack and a ranged attack. Units are often specialized to be better using one attack type. All units also have stances; these affect how the units respond to enemies. There are three types of units: commanders, infantry, and vehicles.

Commanders are hero units, and can usually only be built once. If they perish, they may be rebuilt. A sub-class is the semi-commander unit, which has many abilities like the commander unit but may be built multiple times. Infantry are foot soldiers, and may either be regular or heavy, with heavy infantry being much tougher than normal infantry. Vehicles are heavy weaponry and transports, and include tanks, artillery and troop carriers.

All units, aside from most heroes and vehicles, come in squads. These are groups of infantry that are commanded as a single entity. They may be reinforced with additional members, equipped with special weapons, or be attached to hero units. Some squads have special abilities, such as grenades, teleportation, and stealth, unlocked with research or leader units. Unit longevity is determined by their health and morale points, which govern a squad's fighting effectiveness. Both are reduced by weaponry; morale recharges independently or due to unit abilities, while health is increased by healer units or repair.

Buildings

Aside from their initial headquarters, races may build research and resource centers, unit-producing facilities, and defensive fortifications. Research buildings may research special upgrades that increase the abilities of that race's units, while resource buildings produce resources. Unit facilities produce infantry and vehicles. In order to access their next tier, a race must build certain buildings to unlock new technologies and buildings.

Multiplayer

Players may either connect directly by IP connection, play on their LAN or play through an online service offered by Gamespy. There are eight game modes available for online skirmish play, such as Annihilate, which requires the player to destroy every enemy unit and building, or Sudden Death, which causes a player to be eliminated if another captures one of their strategic points. Multiple game modes may be enabled, calling for multiple winning conditions. Due to its nature as a standalone expansion pack, the player may only play as the Tau or Necrons in multi-player. They must enter their original Dawn of War CD key to gain access to the original four races. Likewise, a Winter Assault CD key is needed to access the Imperial Guard.

Campaign

The expansion features a "Risk-based strategic layer", which is a campaign including a "meta-map" of the world of Kronus, the game's setting, similar to those in Westwood Studios' Dune games as opposed to the old, programmed, linear storylines common in most real-time strategy games. Due to this lack of linearity, there is no plot aside from the opening cinematic; rather, as the player conquers various opposing factions for control of Kronus the game provides a narrative specific to whom the player has conquered. If the player wins the campaign, a cinematic is played that depends on which faction the player was controlling. With the release of Dawn of War II, it is learned that the Blood Ravens (Space Marines) were officially the victorious faction, and that they were the army that both defeated the Imperial Guard present on Kronus at the city of Victory Bay and the Word Bearers Chaos Space Marines who had sought to conquer the world. It is left ambiguous whether or not the Blood Ravens were the faction that eliminated the other combatants.

The player may pick a faction to play as, and then engages in turn-based combat with the other A.I.-controlled factions. There are multiple provinces, which are conquered by fighting a regular skirmish match over them. These may give either a special bonus or supply special 'honour guard' units, which are powerful, non-trainable versions of regular units. They may only be made on the main battlefield overview screen, and, like provincial reinforcements, cost planetary requisition, a resource gained on a per-turn basis based on how many provinces the player controls. Honour guard units transfer over provinces and may be used repeatedly. There are also seven 'Stronghold' provinces that function as bases for the respective factions; these are made like more traditional campaign games, with multiple secondary and primary objectives that vary from faction to faction.

Also, a race's commander unit may be upgraded with special "wargear", unique, customizable upgrades that vary by race. These are awarded at battle milestones, ranging from a certain amount of kills to conquering many provinces.

Campaign scenarios are persistent, meaning that all player structures are 'saved' when a game is won. For example, if a player builds a base and conquers a province, only to have a neighbouring faction attack aforementioned province, the player will start out with his previous base, with the exception of having no technology researched. Players may also choose to garrison provinces with units that are instantly available should the province be attacked. These are bought with planetary requisition.

Setting

All the pre-existing factions of the Dawn of War series gained new units in Dark Crusade, and two new playable races are made available, including the following.

T'au Empire

One of the two new races in the game, the T'au are unique in multiple ways. T'au Fire Warriors and their anti-gravity vehicles are powerful in ranged battles, but have weak melee capability. To compensate, they fight alongside the alien avian Kroot, using these primal warriors as auxiliaries. 

Also, they are the only race with a "choice" in their final technology; two final tier buildings are presented, which provide different end-game units and technologies. Only one may be chosen. 

The T'au also do not have any standalone defensive structures or minefields.

Necrons

The Necrons are unique, as they do not require requisition to build their army. Rather, power is the only resource needed to make Necron units. However, capturing Strategic Points and building Obelisks (the Necrons' listening post) will increase the speed of research and building, and will also expand the population cap. 

The Necron Monolith, their home base, is restored as more buildings are built, while also unlocking new units (similar to how the Zerg function in Starcraft), and functions as the only vehicle- and troop-producing building for the faction. 

When fully restored, the Monolith becomes mobile (albeit very slow, though it can teleport) and is armed with very powerful Gauss weaponry. 

Many Necron units have the ability to resurrect, and most will leave persistent corpses on the battlefield that may either self-resurrect after they die or be restored by specialized Necron units.

The Necrons were previously seen in Dawn of War - Winter Assault at the end of the single-player campaign, but were noticeably stronger.

Notable Characters

Dark Crusade features some reappearances by characters from the past games in the series, such as Farseer Taldeer and Warboss Gorgutz 'Ead 'Unter, who were featured in Winter Assault.

Plot

Setting

The events of Dark Crusade take place sometime after the Damocles Gulf Crusade on the former Imperial Civilised World of Kronus, a planet on the Eastern Fringe of the Imperium in the Segmentum Ultima, where the local Human population has been absorbed into the nearby T'au Empire. There is also a local Feral Ork population in the southern jungles and frozen northern regions of the main continent on Kronus. 

These populations were all unaware that the planet is also an ancient Necron Tomb World, although the Humans and T'au had identified the ruins of "an unknown ancient civilisation" which were scattered at various points across the planet. In addition, Kronus happened to be a world with a variety of hidden Imperial relics, left behind from the Horus Heresy, that are significant to the Blood Ravens Space Marines, the Imperial Guard, and the Word Bearers Traitor Legion of Chaos Space Marines.

Initial Dispositions

Since the events of the Damocles Gulf Crusade in the Lithesh Sector of the Eastern Fringe, when elements of the Novamarines' 4th Company and two companies of the Astra Militarum's 167th Brimlock Dragoons attempted to combat the growing influence of the T'au Empire on the planet, Kronus had a been a largely peaceful and successful Mining World that was home to Human, T'au and Kroot populations. The departure of the planet's governing Ethereals 200 standard years prior to the Dark Crusade signalled that the world was firmly under T'au rule. While there was guerrilla resistance from Imperial loyalists and fanatical Emperor-worshippers, the three species coexisted largely harmoniously, although tensions sometimes flared up into riots and violence. 

The only major threats present on the planet were nomadic Human raiders who lived in the central plains, as well as repeated attacks by the Feral Ork tribes that controlled parts of the southern jungles and the frozen north of the world's primary settled continent. Nonetheless, the T'au forces present on the planet were enough to keep these threats, and the few Human dissidents, under control.

It's unclear how aware the T'au were of the Chaos Cults present on Kronus. Certainly the cults were minor enough not to pose an existential threat to T'au rule. However, these cults, a by-product of the various unholy relics deposited on the planet during, or in the aftermath of, the Horus Heresy, had a long foothold on Kronus. Various "cults of the gun" -- groups that worshipped a Great Unclean One trapped within a Hellstorm Cannon that had been severed from a Chaos Titan during the Horus Heresy, and brought to the abandoned former capital of Ironworks Bay by the Tech-priests of Mars -- had existed on Kronus for many standard millennia. Various Chaos temples and ritual sites were still present in the southern regions of the world's primary landmass, largely hidden from prying eyes by the jungles. There Chaos-worshippers gathered in secret, and performed blasphemous acts in the Dark Gods' names.

The inciting incident for the Dark Crusade came when a local Human archaeological team unearthed a mysterious crypt on the Thur'abis plateau in Kronus' central desert. Exploring the crypt led the archaeologists underground into the catacombs of the Necrons who were still lost to the Great Sleep, and through some unknown means, they awakened them. The Necrons killed the archaeologists, and converted their leader, Thomas Macabee, into a Pariah. The Necron Lord of Kronus ordered his warriors to begin a campaign of extermination on behalf of the C'tan Shard known as the Nightbringer, and rid the Tomb World of all of its "lesser" species. Macabee somehow retained some of his personality after his transformation, and occasionally spoke for the otherwise silent Necrons as they advanced.

Around the same time, a Chaos Cult of the Deimos Peninsula succeeded in summoning a company of Word Bearers Chaos Space Marines to Kronus. Many of them, including their leader, Dark Apostle Eliphas the Inheritor, were veterans of the battles fought on Kronus during the Horus Heresy. The Word Bearers raised unholy temples dedicated to the Chaos Gods across the peninsula, corrupting the land in the process. Their goal was to convert the planet into a Daemon World from which they could strike deeper into the Imperium. For Eliphas, the stakes were higher: this war would be his proving ground from which he hoped to gain favour with the Dark Gods. More specifically, this meant the favour of Tzeentch, for destroying the Necrons, and Nurgle for reawakening the Great Unclean One within the Hellstorm Cannon that was now located at Ironworks Bay.

In the wake of the Necrons' attacks, the planet's inhabitants sent a distress call to the T'au Empire, seeking the Fire Caste's aid against the new threat. While this missive did reach the T'au government, it was also intercepted by the Ork Warboss Gorgutz 'Ead 'Unter whose forces had been raiding worlds on the T'au Empire's outskirts. Sensing that Kronus was vulnerable to attack, Gorgutz marshalled his WAAAGH! for an invasion of the planet. 

The T'au government was unwilling to tolerate having a thriving colony taken from the Greater Good, so the Ethereals dispatched a massive force of T'au Fire Caste Warriors, supported by Kroot and Vespid auxiliaries, to retake the planet, and push back the invaders. The force was under the overall command of the Ethereal Aun'el Shi'Ores, and led militarily by T'au Commander Shas'O Kais, both of whom were chosen for this mission for their skill and intelligence.

The Necrons' return had been foreseen by the Eldar Farseer Taldeer of Craftworld Ulthwé, who mobilised her forces to fight the Eldar's ancient enemy. With Kronus beyond the reach of the Webway, the Eldar strike force Taldeer assembled was forced to arrive using the wraithship Vision of Lileath, which hid at the edge of the Kronus System. A small shuttle was dispatched to Tyrea, a frozen region far from the local civilisation on the main continent, where they erected special Webway gates linked to the Vision of Lileath's hold, allowing the Eldar to deploy to the planet. 

By the time the Eldar arrived on Kronus, fighting between the Necrons and the other powers present had already begun. Unable to trust the other intelligent species on the planet to deal with the Necrons appropriately, Taldeer abandoned her usual strategy of misdirection and guerilla warfare, and ordered her followers to undertake the outright conquest of Kronus. Only in this way could the Eldar ensure the Necrons' destruction, though the cost in precious Eldar lives would be higher. 

Despite the Eldar's use of their cloaking technology to hide their arrival, T'au Pathfinders operating in the Arel Highlands south of Tyrea detected their technology and alerted the local T'au Fire Caste forces. The Eldar's appearance was also another opportunity for Eliphas to increase his power and standing with the Chaos Gods, by offering the Eldar's souls to Slaanesh.

Taldeer's appearance on the planet drew an Astra Militarum force to Kronus, which had been pursuing Taldeer ever since her manipulations of the 412th Cadian Shock Trooper Regiment on Lorn V cost the Imperium a chance to recover an Imperator-class Battle Titan. When these troops made planetfall at Ironworks Bay, they discovered the ancient Hellstorm Cannon within the city's borders. When their leader, General Lukas Alexander, reported this find to his superiors at Segmentum Command, they promoted him to Governor-Militant of Kronus, and ordered him to recover any other relics of the Tech-priests, and bring the planet back under Imperial control. 

In the spirit of this task, Alexander renamed the the city of Ironworks Bay as "Victory Bay", while his troops, now reorganised as the 1st Kronus Regiment, adopted the sobriquet "Liberators". Accompanying the Liberators were a collection of Tech-priests, among them the Magos Explorator Caius who sought the records of Magos Palladius, a Tech-priest who studied the Hellstorm Cannon in the aftermath of the Horus Heresy.

To support the Liberators' operations the Navis Imperialis set up a blockade around Kronus. However, the blockade was compromised when O Kais' reinforcements reached Kronus. The vessels of the T'au Protection Fleet managed to break through the blockade, and land troops in the planet's capital city, Asharis, which the xenos renamed Tash'n, making it their headquarters in their efforts to restore order to the planet. With the T'au's arrival, Eliphas now had a way of gaining Lorgar's approval, as the Daemon Primarch hated the irreligious above all others. 

As the battle between the two navies continued, the ships of WAAAGH! Gorgutz dropped out of the Warp above Kronus. Caught in the crossfire between the two fleets, the sheer number of Roks Gorgutz launched at Kronus' surface ensured that some would make it to the planet, including the one carrying the warboss himself. 

The Roks crashed into the Green Coast on the main continent, destroying several Feral Ork settlements in the process, and unloaded the Orks who had survived the space battle. A fierce combat broke out between Gorgutz's Boyz and the local feral tribes, which ended with Gorgutz imposing his authority over the entire region. The most technologically capable among the combined Ork force set to work stockpiling weapons and building vehicles in anticipation of Gorgutz's conquest of the planet. Eliphas marked Gorgutz's arrival, as the warboss still carried the skull of the World Eaters' Chaos Lord Crull on his trophy rack since the Ork had killed him on Lorn V. The skull would make a fitting offering to Khorne.

The final force to arrive on Kronus was a Blood Ravens Space Marine task force consisting of elements of the 1st, 2nd, and 5th Companies, under the command of 5th Company Captain Davian Thule who served as the mission's Force Commander. The force had been dispatched by Azariah Kyras, the Blood Ravens' Chapter Master and Chief Librarian, because he had discovered that relics relating to the Chapter's mysterious early history were to be found there, and needed to be protected. Kyras ordered Thule to purge the planet of all enemies of the Imperium. 

Arriving aboard the battle barge Litany of Fury, the Space Marines established the fortress known as the Castellum Incorruptus in North Vandea on the main continent, creating a forward base from which they could call reinforcements down from orbit. Thule sent a stand-down order to the 1st Kronus Liberators, but Alexander refused to yield, arguing that his orders came from Segmentum Command, and thus superseded the Space Marines' authority. Thule judged Alexander to be a good man, but, without any other choice, he ordered his forces to fight the Astra Militarum as well as the xenos and Heretics present on the planet, something they ultimately did without reluctance or mercy. The Blood Ravens' arrival also gave Eliphas a chance to earn Abaddon the Despoiler's favour for himself, for the Warmaster of Chaos desired the defeat of the Blood Ravens for reasons known only to himself.

Variant Endings

The game has various endings for all the different factions, depending on who the player picks to play as and leads to victory. Information gleaned from the narratives of Dawn of War II, Dawn of War II - Chaos Rising, Dawn of War II - Retribution and Dawn of War III, reveals that the Blood Ravens were the canonical victor of the Dark Crusade, rendering the other six of the game's endings non-canonical, including those below.

Eliphas the Inheritor and the Forces of Chaos: *Eliphas the Inheritor and the Forces of Chaos: If Eliphas won the campaign, he ascended to Daemonhood. Kronus is transformed into the newest Daemon World in the galaxy, its population either converted to Chaos worship, or sacrificed in their rituals. Eliphas' next act is to gather Chaos warfleets at Kronus, while his sorcerers summoned legions of Daemons to bolster their ranks. The subsequent campaign launched from Kronus cut through much of Ultima Segmentum and pushed towards Terra, heralding the Imperium's darkest days.

Taldeer and the Eldar of Craftworld Ulthwé: If Taldeer won, she and her forces abandoned Kronus and vanished into space to an unknown fate. They left behind a planet in anarchy, which resulted in Kronus becoming a lawless backwater for many standard centuries. Imperial scholars speculated as to whether this was what Taldeer had intended all along, and that her true goal was to prevent any of the other six factions from taking control of Kronus. 

Lukas Alexander and the Imperial Guard - If Governor-Militant Alexander won the campaign, he abdicated his command of the 1st Kronus Liberators and promoted Captain Vash to colonel of the regiment in his stead so that he could devote himself fully to ruling the planet. The troops he commanded remained on Kronus for a "pacification period", before shipping out to the next Imperial conflict zone, although their personal loyalty to, and hero worship of Alexander remained. A vast number of Tech-priests arrived on Kronus with resources to undertake a great campaign of public works, in return for gaining access to the Hellstorm Cannon at Victory Bay, and the opportunity to return it to active use. All this gave further power to Alexander who rose to become one of the most powerful Imperial Commanders in the Lithesh Sector.

Necron Lord of Kronus and the Necrons - If the Necron Lord won control of Kronus, he used the Necrons' technology to scour the planet's ecosystems, returning it to the uncontested status of a Tomb World once more. The Imperial, T'au and Eldar forces eventually attempted to retake Kronus, but all fell to the Necron crypt vessels now protecting the planet in orbit. Kronus became the centre of the Necrons' purge of the "lesser races" in the eastern reaches of the galaxy, and their warriors devastated planets in the T'au Empire, as well as in the Realm of Ultramar, and many other sectors.

Gorgutz 'Ead 'Unter and the Orks - If Gorgutz won the campaign, he gained a new epithet in Ork kultur for each of the other factions he defeated, and pillaged the planet to build a new Greenskin invasion fleet, before leaving Kronus behind in search of new battles. Kronus remained a fertile Ork world to which Gorgutz regularly returned to gather reinforcements for his next war. With these resources at his command, Gorgutz's WAAAGH! ravaged the T'au Empire and the Imperial worlds of the Eastern Fringe for many standard years.

Shas'O Kais and the T'au Empire - If Shas'O Kais won back control of Kronus, he and his task force remained on the planet to secure it, while waves of new T'au and Kroot colonists arrived to resettle the planet. Kronus' Human population, which had largely embraced the return of the Imperium, were subject to gendered re-education camps, and various penalties, as a result. Ten standard years after the T'au victory, the Human population of the world had dropped to 5% of the planet's total populace, partly because of the arrival of the new T'au colonists, but also due to the establishment of the camps and the T'au sterilisation programs initiated within them, that caused the Human birth rate to plummet. By the time Kronus was made a full Sept of the Third Sphere Expansion, the Human population remaining on Kronus was negligible.

Canonical Ending

Relic Entertainment has stated that the Blood Ravens defeated both the Necrons and the Imperial Guard in the canonical ending. Through the dialogue in Dawn of War II, it is apparent that the Blood Ravens were able to conquer Kronus and return control of it to the Imperium. Below is a description of the Blood Ravens march to victory in the in-game campaign supplemented by details revealed in the later Dawn of War games.

The Dark Crusade was a bloody and hard-fought conflict, marked by slow, methodical, ground combat. Any attempt to use air power or Assault Craft to travel the planet more quickly was thwarted by the myriad of fleets in orbit, who shot down any aircraft that sought to transport troops. That situation only changed with the Battle of Pavonis, when the Blood Ravens took control of Kronus' spaceport from the T'au. The combination of Dark Age and Air Caste technology present at the port enabled them to plot routes that now bypassed the fleets' defensive positions, enabling them to strike freely across the planet.

Battle of Victory Bay

To end the conflict with the 1st Kronus Liberators of the Imperial Guard, the Space Marines advanced on their capital at Victory Bay, setting up camp on the southern banks of the city's Victoria River, directly across the river from the Governor's Palace. Before the Blood Ravens launched their attack, Davian Thule offered Governor-Militant Lukas Alexander one last chance to withdraw his troops to orbit, and resolve their conflict between the Emperor's servants peacefully, but Alexander steadfastly refused. 

Over the course of the battle that ensued, the Blood Ravens fought their way through the Imperial Guard's fortified bases on the south bank of the Victoria River, before crossing the river and silencing their artillery. The Astartes also captured the Hellstorm Cannon, which Alexander had reactivated to use against the Space Marines, and converted it to their own use. 

Ultimately, Blood Ravens levelled the Governor's Palace, killing Alexander in the process. With their commander's death, the 1st Kronus surrendered to the Blood Ravens, but a handful of Tech-priests and Kasrkin who were defending the Hellstorm Cannon, in a final act of defiance, overloaded the massive weapon's core, destroying it and preventing it from passing into the Blood Ravens' hands. 

In the aftermath of the battle, Thule and his Reclusiarch Mikelus praised Alexander's devotion to his orders, and bestowed upon the Liberators testimonials to their bravery, before arranging safe passage for them back to Segmentum Command. The only guardsmen not to receive this treatment was the 5th Company which had turned on their superiors and fought alongside the Space Marines, who the Blood Ravens executed en masse as traitors to their regiment.

Battle of Tyrea

Eager to avenge their battle brothers who had fallen to Eldar manipulations on Tartarus and Rahe's Paradise, the Blood Ravens tracked down the Elda's hidden base in the Tyrea region of Kronus's main continent. Recognising that her forces were not intended to fight a siege, but unable to abandon the Webway Gates that linked Kronus to the Eldar warship Vision of Lileath in orbit, Farseer Taldeer had her Warlocks lure a force of dissatisfied Orks that had broken off from Gorgutz's WAAAGH! to Tyrea to cut off any attacker's supply lines. 

When the Space Marines attacked, she baited them into another conflict with a splinter cult of Chaos Space Marines. Thus engaged, the Blood Ravens fought their way through their old enemies, and defeated the Ork and Chaos threat.

Taldeer hoped that these engagements would weaken the Blood Ravens to the point that her own forces could defeat them, spearheaded by the might of an Avatar of Khaine. In this she was proven wrong, as the Space Marines were able to push onto the hill where the Eldar had established their primary base, and take and hold the three Webway Gates. 

After various attempts to retake them failed, the Eldar's defeat was signalled by two separate events that happened almost at once: the Blood Ravens' destruction of the central gate using the firepower of their Whirlwinds, and the death of the Avatar of Khaine at the hands of Epistolary Anteas, the leader of Davian Thule's Librarians. 

Cut off from further reinforcements from the Vision of Lileath, and with their only avenue of escape closed to them, the remaining Eldar melted away into the countryside of the main continent. By the time the Blood Ravens reached the heart of the Eldar's base, only Taldeer herself remained, attacking the Blood Ravens with her weapons and potent psychic abilities. The Space Marines defeated her, capturing her alive rather than killing her.

Purge of Tash'n

Davian Thule's strategy to finish off the T'au on Kronus was not to conquer the T'au capital at Tash'n, the former Imperial city of Asharis, but instead to break the T'au's will to fight by assassinating their leader Aun'El Shi'Ores. Shas'O Kais, predicting his adversary's plan, had his Ethereal pre-emptively moved to a citadel at the heart of Tash'n with his Fire Caste bodyguard, forcing the Blood Ravens to fight their way through the T'au positions in order to reach him.

The Blood Ravens fought block-by-block in bitter street fighting, repelling strike teams of Fire Warriors and Kroot Carnivores, and weathering ambushes by XV15 and XV25 Stealth Teams that attacked weak points in their formations. While they advanced with steely determination, momentum shifted in the Space Marines' favour when they captured Unity Plaza, the T'au's only strategic vulnerability. The advanced communications array present there enabled the Blood Ravens to detect, and thus eliminate, the Fire Caste Stealth Teams, freeing them up to advance further into Tash'n.

After destroying the T'au command posts the Blood Ravens were able to attack the citadel, and kill Shi'Ores, crushing the morale of the T'au army. Shas'O Kais led a team of XV8 Crisis Battlesuits to successfully extract the Ethereal's corpse from the citadel, planning to take the body to be buried on his homeworld of T'au. With nothing else to fight for, O Kais ordered a withdrawal of all T'au forces from Kronus, leaving Or'es Tash'n to be reclaimed for the Imperium by the Space Marines.

Battle Beneath the Thur'abis Plateau

The Blood Ravens did not understand the true strength of the Necrons until they sent Scout Marines to raid the catacombs under the Thur'Abis Plateau where the Necrons had first appeared. The garbled reports that the scouts sent back before they were killed described a whole network of tunnels and crypts, with many Necron Warriors still to be awakened from the Great Sleep. Unable to fight the seemingly endless numbers of Necrons, Davian Thule settled on a desperate gambit: he would lead a strike force into the catacombs, plant a bomb in the main cavern, and escape before the ordnance exploded and collapsed the plateau on top of the Necrons, burying them forever. 

Despite the dark and fearsome location, the Blood Ravens fought their way through the catacombs, destroying Necron beacons (which could cause different effects on the Space Marines or their enemies, such as reviving fallen Necrons or causing the Blood Ravens' vehicle units to turn on them) as they worked their way towards a key structural point within the tomb complex. There, Thule, who had been carrying the bomb himself, placed the weapon and set the timer before ordering the retreat. During the battle, Thule and Mikelus duelled the Necron Lord of Kronus, thoiugh the fight resulted in Mikelus' death, and Thule lost an eye before he managed to defeat the undying creature. 

As the Necrons began their pursuit of the retreating Space Marines, they realised the true danger too late to stop it. The bomb exploded, entombing the Necrons once more beneath millions of tons of rock. The Space Marines who survived the battle marked their power armour with an elongated skull, symbolising their victory over the Necrons. The Blood Ravens' 2nd Company, which provided most of the support in this operation, later incorporated the skull motif into their company heraldry, and took the motto "Victory Over Death".

War for the Deimos Plateau

The Chaos Space Marines of the Word Bearers Traitor Legion, under the orders of the Dark Apostle Eliphas the Inheritor, were able to rip open a hole in the Warp and construct a gate around it to keep it stable. This allowed Chaos Daemons to pour through the rift and corrupt the land beneath their feet, as well as erect large pillars designed to draw on the energies of Chaos sorcery and sacrifice any willing or unwilling beings that ventured too close to further power their arcane strength. 

In the defence of the Warp Gate that had brought the Chaos forces to Kronus, Eliphas decided to allow his troops to defend their own sections of a long, twisted path to the gate instead of focusing on the defence of the region as a whole. However, because he was unable to command his defences effectively, the Blood Ravens cut their way through the bloody gauntlet of Chaos Space Marines and demolished the Warp Gate. Eliphas, having failed his Daemonic masters one too many times, was sacrificed by the will of the Ruinous Powers as a failed subject, his soul drawn wailing back into the Warp.

Yet Eliphas returned in Dawn of War II - Chaos Rising to bedevil the Blood Ravens once more after having been resurrected following much punishment by the Chaos Gods and placed in thrall to the Black Legion at the request of Abaddon the Despoiler. Eliphas promised the Despoiler and the Ruinous Powers that his only mission in life after his resurrection was to destroy the Blood Ravens as a Chapter.

Green Coast Campaign

The Blood Ravens now faced the daunting prospect of attempting to drive the Orks out of Kronus' southern jungles, something Commander Farsight had failed to do. With enemies now so close to home, and defeat looming, the chieftains of the tribes that made up Warboss Gorgutz 'Ead 'Unter's WAAAGH! began to wonder whether they might make better leaders than Gorgutz. Gorgutz managed to maintain control by building huge banners in the tribes' settlements as a symbol of his authority, but his only practical solution to this discontent was to prove his strength by defeating the attacking Space Marines. 

The Blood Ravens used the inherent dissent of the Ork tribes to their advantage by attacking each settlement in sequence and destroying the banner in each one, causing the tribes to begin openly defying Gorgutz's authority, and start fighting amongst each other. Fighting their way through the carnage unleashed by the Ork civil war, the Blood Ravens enhanced the chaos by killing the Squiggoth trainers of the Footstompaz tribe, and letting the untrained monstrosities loose to wreak havoc across the battlefield. 

Eventually the Blood Ravens closed in on Gorgutz and his loyalists. Sensing his defeat was inevitable, the warboss set off a massive bomb inside his camp that killed Space Marines and Orks alike. In the confusion of the explosions, Gorgutz escaped through a tunnel he'd dug before the battle, and was able to flee Kronus on a hidden Ork voidship.

With the battle won, the Blood Ravens conducted a scorched earth campaign across the Green Coast, burning thousands of square kilometres of jungle to ensure the Orks' complete extermination. 

Aftermath

Even after the Blood Ravens completed their purge of Kronus, the 5th Company maintained control of its fortress at the Castellum, and kept the battle barge Litany of Fury in close orbit, preventing all access to the planet. In addition to the ancient relics of its Founding the Chapter had been sent to recover, the Blood Ravens had also acquired T'au blood samples, the remains of an Avatar of Khaine, as well as a catalogue of the Chaos artefacts and temples of the Deimos Peninsula. While these objects were shipped to the Chapter's librarium for careful study, the Word Bearers' artefacts were safely destroyed once they were in the Librarians' hands. The Avatar's remains were notable for causing no small amount of trouble for the Chapter in subsequent years.

Despite these triumphs, the Blood Ravens' victory was not as complete as they would have liked. Shas'O Kais' actions had denied the Chapter's Apothecaries an Ethereal's body to dissect, and they had failed to secure Victory Bay's Hellstorm Cannon for the Chapter, much to their anger. The sole surviving enginseer in Victory Bay was interrogated by the furious Blood Ravens for the reason why they had destroyed the cannon, only to learn that he had done it to prevent the cannon from "falling into unworthy hands." 

The Chapter's failure to kill Gorgutz also caused Thule some concern. He believed that the warboss would return to threaten the Imperium again. Time would prove him correct in this, as Gorgutz returned to battle the Blood Ravens, and other Imperial forces, during the Kaurava Campaign several standard years later.

The Blood Ravens' systematic hunt for the remaining Eldar on the planet was similarly unsuccessful. Taldeer's sacrifice had given her forces the time needed to hide in the wilderness of Tyrea, where they remained undetected for standard years afterwards. Their salvation came thanks to the prophecies of Craftworld Alaitoc, one of which contained a method of evacuating those Eldar still hiding in Kronus' glaciers. 

Unwilling to risk drawing the attention of the Blood Ravens, Alaitoc's seers acted in secret. Guided by the seers' visions, the Eldar recovery from Kronus proved a stunning success, and resulted in the recovery of nearly all of the spirit stones on the planet, which had been buried under several standard years of snowfall. The only spirit stone they never found was Taldeer's, which was with her when she was captured by the Blood Ravens. It remained with her during her subsequent interrogation, which was overseen by Chapter Master Azariah Kyras himself. After learning all he could from her, Kyras oversaw her execution, and kept her spirit stone on his person as a trophy, thus unknowingly preventing it from returning to Taldeer's people.

Although the Blood Ravens had won the Dark Crusade, their actions would make their lives more difficult in the long term. The Blood Ravens' willingness to fight the Astra Militarum was noted even as the war began, and rumours circulated that the relics that the Blood Ravens sought were in fact unholy, rather than blessed. More accusations were levelled at the Chapter when the remaining guardsmen of the 1st Kronus Liberators reached, and reported to, Segmentum Command. 

Segmentum Command protested the Blood Ravens' actions on Kronus and reported them to the Inquisition. The Blood Ravens successfully defended themselves by arguing that Lukas Alexander should have submitted to the traditionally superior authority of the Adeptus Astartes, and withdrawn as ordered, but the accusations of hoarded relics never fully went away, and the Inquisition kept a close watch on the Blood Ravens thereafter. Furthermore, many members of the Astra Militarum never forgot what the Blood Ravens did to their soldiers during the Dark Crusade.

Regardless of the great victory achieved by the Blood Ravens, with the Battle of Thur'abis Plateau in particular becoming a legend among the Chapter's battle-brothers, the Dark Crusade was remembered ultimately as the beginning of the Chapter's darkest days, The double blow of the militarily disastrous Kaurava Campaign and Hive Fleet Leviathan's invasion of the Chapter's recruiting worlds in Sub-sector Aurelia, which unleashed the three Aurelian Crusades, came soon after the Dark Crusade's conclusion.

Development

Dark Crusade uses the same engine as the original Dawn of War game, which allowed the game's developer, Relic, to focus most efforts on revamping the single-player campaign and balancing out the two new races. By E3 2006, the two new races were demonstrated through an in-engine feature, though no playable version was showcased. New units were unveiled regularly after August 11, 2006. The first available playable build was released on September 18 to GameSpot. Dark Crusade exited the development stage on September 21, and was expected to be shipped worldwide by October 9, 2006.

Reception

The expansion was praised as an "excellent real-time strategy game", with its non-linear single-player campaign, stabilized multiplayer and additional features, and was selected by IGN and Gamespot as the best expansion pack of 2006. Most were quite surprised by the quality and size of the expansion, stating that the large amount of new content "breathed new life into the game". Critics cited the excellent balancing between races, saying it had a positive effect on both single-player and multiplayer. Aside from new units and races, changes to the gameplay mechanics, such as reworking of the elite unit system, were well-received, as critics felt that the changes "forced players to actually think about using real combined arms tactics in multiplayer and contributed to more games decided by genuine strategy and skill rather than just who can spec out the proper build order."

Some critics stated that the game's weak points included its complexity; some reviewers disliked how much micromanagement was required to effectively field one race against another, saying that this complexity was detrimental to the game. As one reviewer mentioned "...when I stop having fun, I stop playing." The learning curve was also stated as being very steep for an RTS, due to each race being different. Another thing critics disliked was the player's inability to 'zoom out' with the in-game camera; this sometimes got in the way of large battles, and was especially noticeable when playing as or against the Tau, as their long range weapons often resulted in them engaging their targets off-screen. While general consensus on the revamped single-player campaign was positive, some critics felt that it was not particularly challenging; they found that the AI was strategically weak, and there were no random elements in most skirmish missions that would enhance replayability. The usage of a random auto-resolve feature to determine which races won which territories was seen as a downside; also, one critic found that the AI never attacked his homebase, and that they would attack insignificant zones regardless of the opportunity to capture more significant ones. In terms of new additions, some critics felt that the races, in particular the Necrons, were overpowered. Despite these accusations (or perhaps because of them), the Tau and Necrons remain some of the least-used races in multi-player games.

Notes

Due to the freeform nature of Dawn of War: Dark Crusade's campaign, there is no canonical order for the fall of each faction's stronghold fell, or even if the Blood Ravens were responsible for all of them as the canonical victors of the campaign. They are presented above in geographical distance from the Space Marines' start position in the province of North Vandea on Kronus, and the events are recounted as if the Blood Ravens destroyed all the other factions themselves.
