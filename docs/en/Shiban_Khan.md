Shiban Khan was a Chogoris-born khan (captain) of the White Scars Legion's Brotherhood of the Storm during the Great Crusade and Horus Heresy. 

History

Shiban, once known as Tamu of the Khin-zan tribe, was born beneath the wind-swept peaks of the Talskar region on Chogoris, a land of vast skies, galloping thunder, and blood-honed tradition. At just twelve local years of age (eight by the standard Terran count), he was discovered amid the smoke and stillness of a tribal battle by the Stormseer Targutai Yesugei. His life was spared, and his path changed. 

Upon induction into the Vth Legion, the boy laid down the name of Tamu and took up the mantle of Shiban, a brother of the storm. He was folded into the brotherhood (company) of Hasik Noyan-Khan, where the forge of war would begin to shape him into something far harder -- and far more sorrowful -- than mere flesh and bone. In time, Shiban rose to command the Brotherhood of the Storm, his loyalty to Jaghatai Khan unwavering. But the Horus Heresy tore at even the strongest bonds. 

As the primarch walked the scorched ruins of Prospero, Shiban uncovered a conspiracy within the White Scars: a schism led by his once-brother-in-arms, Torghun Khan, and the ambitious Hasik Noyan-Khan, seeking to turn the Legion's blade toward the cause of the Warmaster Horus. With fire in his blood and duty in his heart, Shiban seized his command aboard the Legion flagship Swordstorm, intercepting the Traitors before they could make a pact with the Death Guard. His stand bought Jaghatai Khan precious time to return -- and set the White Scars' course back to loyalty to the Emperor in the Second Battle of Prospero.

The cost of such devotion, however, is never light. Four long standard years of brutal civil war transformed Shiban in flesh and spirit. Limbs torn in attrition were replaced with cold augmetics -- he called them his "Shackles," as if they were the weight of his failures. His laughter, once bold as the wind on the steppe, died. His warriors called him Tachseer -- "the Restorer" -- for he took in loyal sons cast out from rebellious brotherhoods and reforged them beneath his grim command. 

But bitterness festered. When he met Torghun Khan again during the mustering at the ancient device known as the Dark Glass, there was no reconciliation -- only condemnation. Shiban spat the word "Traitor" and sought to throw his former brother into the teeth of the enemy.

At the Catallus Warp Rift during the Battle of Catallus, fate drew blood once more. Shiban fell to the blades of Ravasch Cario, a Palatine Blades prefector of the Emperor's Children. As the killing stroke descended, the Traitor's own Daemonic corruption was unleashed and prevented him from landing the blow. Seizing the moment, Shiban struck with righteous fury and slew the prefector. But the wounds ran deeper than flesh. 

During the Siege of Terra, Shiban was assigned to defend the Imperial Palace's Eternity Wall Spaceport beside battle-hardened allies: the steadfast Camba-Diaz of the Imperial Fists, High Primary Solar General Saul Niborran, and the fierce Sister of Silence, Jenetia Krole. There, amidst the maelstrom, he found strange solace in Captain Al-Nid Nazira of the Imperial Army -- a mortal whose courage in the face of such carnage and insanity stirred something long buried.

In the final stages of the battle, the Eternity Wall Spaceport was overrun by the Heretic Astartes of the World Eaters. Shiban was determined to help evacuate some of his Imperial Army comrades. However, as the shuttle transporting them took off, they were assailed by an assault force of World Eaters. Though he had taken a vow to never take a step back and die in defence of the spaceport, Nazira's risk of his own life to save Shiban and the idea of using the shuttle's anti-grav systems to kill many of the Traitors spurred Shiban to turn around and head back to defend the shuttlecraft. Barely fighting off the massing World Eaters so that he could leap onto the already departing shuttle, Shiban discovered that Nazira and the pilot had already been slain by Traitor bolter fire as the aircraft crashed into the Eternity Wall itself.

Shiban survived the crash, but was horrifically wounded and stranded far from the Loyalist lines. Slowly making his way back to the walls of the Imperial Palace, he was plagued by visions of Targutai Yesugei and Torghun Khan. Shiban eventually came upon a surviving Imperial Army lieutenant named Cole, who was desperately trying to save an abandoned infant. Seeing this act as part of his fate, Shiban pledged to deliver Cole and the infant to the Inner Palace. Fighting through all kinds of Warp-spawned horrors with nothing but a broken metal pole, a badly-injured Shiban managed to carry the dying Cole as well as the infant back to the walls of the Palace. There, reunited with his Legion, Shiban fought on, quieter than before, his wrath now tempered by something closer to hope -- or at least, meaning.

Now reunited with his Legion, Shiban grimly continued the fight against the Traitors while still yearning to speak to his old friend Torghun Khan one last time. When Jaghatai Khan launched his fabled charge to reclaim the Lion's Gate Spaceport, Shiban was there, blade in hand, stride unbroken. Charged with taking the spaceport's anti-ship orbital batteries, Shiban was not present when Jaghatai Khan disappeared following his victory over the Daemon Primarch Mortarion. 

Following the Khan's apparent death much of the White Scars Legion initiated a ritual death-duel in which they intended to fight to their end. Yet with the Departmento Munitorum attache to the V th Legion Ilya Ravallion at his side, Shiban rallied the remaining White Scars instead, reinforcing the spaceport's defence upon learning their primarch had survived, albeit he had been gravely wounded.

As the Blood Angels Primarch Sanguinius held the Eternity Gate against annihilation by the forces of Chaos, Shiban and his White Scars unleashed a punishing anti-orbital bombardment upon the vast Traitor armada that hung over Terra. In the aftermath, Shiban was named the second Master of the Hunt of the Legion following the death of Jubal Khan, a title earned not in glory, but in scars. His honour was weathered, his soul cracked -- but still, he endured. And so did his blade, until the last storm was called.
