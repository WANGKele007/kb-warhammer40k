The Enumerations are a set of ten meditative mental states devised by the Primarch Magnus the Red during the Great Crusade to help the psykers among his Thousand Sons Legion better control their emotions when drawing upon their abilities.

The higher the Enumeration grade, the more spiritual purity was achieved, as the individual became more free from the corrupting demands of their own ego. 

The Tenth Enumeration was noted as being the highest attainable grade by Ahriman, though it was also said that Magnus himself knew more.

Mastering the Enumerations allowed practitioners of the psychic arts to rise through the societal hierarchy of the people of Prospero, though not all advanced past a certain point. 

There was at least one member of Magnus' inner circle, Hathor Maat, who never attained absolute mastery of the Enumerations.

The Cult of the Thousand Sons known as the Pavoni, the masters of flesh-crafting, had particular difficulty with mastering the higher Enumerations due to the vanity and egocentrism implicit in their chosen psychic discipline.

Known Enumerations

First Enumeration - The First Enumeration was induced by repeating the names of the original masters and cult founders of Tizca. Most mortals are incapable of reaching it according to Ahriman. It cleared one's thoughts and evened out one's emotional peaks.
Second Enumeration - The Second Enumeration enhanced the practice of telepathy by allowing a practicioner to part their mind from their bodily concerns and thus better pursue spiritual enlightenment. This mental purity increased the fidelity of telepathic communications.
Third Enumeration - The Third Enumeration enhanced one's perception of their surroundings.
Fourth Enumeration - The Fourth Enumeration was called upon by Malin Vistario for defensive advance. It was also invoked by Ahzek Ahriman to separate himself from his physical body; its exact purpose is unknown, but it presumably lends itself to psychic reconnaissance.
Fifth Enumeration - The Fifth Enumeration was summoned several times by Ahriman, but its purpose remains unclear. He invoked it in preparation of battle at least once; it allowed him to feel anger and so increase his psychic abilities.
Sixth Enumeration - The Sixth Enumeration which helps a psyker gather their thoughts.
Seventh Enumeration - The Seventh Enumeration which was used by Malin Vistario to unleash enough psychic force to telekinetically lift and assemble a set of heavy stones.
Eighth Enumeration - The Eighth Enumeration serves as a focus for aggression and was employed by most Thousand Sons Legionaries in battle. Not all made use of it, preferring instead to retain the sharpened senses provided by other Enumerations.
Ninth Enumeration - The Ninth Enumeration was described as a sphere of inner determination.
Tenth Enumeration - The Tenth Enumeration was described as a place of inner solitude, peace and absolute detachment. It was also utilised by the Thousand Sons to project their souls into the "Great Ocean" of the Immaterium.

Trivia

The concept of the Pre-Heresy Thousand Sons' Enumerations was likely based on the Jewish mystical tradition of the Kabbalah, specifically the concept of the Ten Sefirot, the Kabbalistic tree of life. 

"Sefirot" directly translates to "enumeration," and its ten emanations mirror the number of the Thousand Sons' Enumerations. 

The inventor of the Enumerations, Graham McNeill, is known to frequently reference real world occultism, as seen in the ranks of the Thousand Sons' cults or the shapes and names of their Tutelaries.
