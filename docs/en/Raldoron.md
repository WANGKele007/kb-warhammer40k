Raldoron, known as "the Blooded" and the "Archein of Wisdom", was the legendary first captain of the Blood Angels Legion during the Great Crusade and Horus Heresy, and later the first Chapter Master of the Blood Angels following the Second Founding. He was one of Primarch Sanguinius' most trusted warriors and carried many honours alongside his command of the elite 1st Company, called "the Protectors", a responsibility hard-won through solar decades of war in the Emperor's name. Raldoron was equerry to his Legion's primarch and held the then-new honourific "Chapter Master", serving in a similar advisory role to Sanguinius as the warriors of Horus' cadre of advisors in the Sons of Horus Legion called the Mournival. 

Raldoron was a Veteran Space Marine captain of the Three Hundred, as the commanders of the 300 companies of the IXth Legion were known, but he was commander of the 1st Company, and his promotion to Chapter Master placed him in a special class of seniority that few other warriors of the Legion could claim. He wore the honour with pride and humility both, as was the Blood Angels' way, but nevertheless, this esteemed rank set him apart from his fellows. He was not a man given to impulsive and ill considered actions and he knew well the hearts and minds of the battle-brothers of the IXth Legion. 

There was some enmity between the Raldoron and Azkaellon, the primarch's herald and commander of the Legion's Sanguinary Guard. Azkaellon was so severe in his manner that oft times it seemed he was utterly inflexible on every question. It was a frequent cause of friction between the warriors of the 1st Company and the Sanguinary Guard. Raldoron's fluid, adaptable command style was at odds with Azkaellon's aloof, rigid comportment, and the two of them reflected their different natures down to the bone.

History

Origins

First among the captains of the Blood Angels' Three Hundred Companies, Raldoron was respected throughout the IXth Legion for his tactical and strategic insight, superb martial skills and faultless discipline. Of all his captains and aides, it is said Sanguinius relied on Raldoron's even temper and keen intelligence more than any other. Even among the other Space Marine Legions, Raldoron's reputation and record were spoken of with respect and admiration. Imperial Fists Primarch Rogal Dorn himself is known to have referred to Raldoron as an "exemplar of the martial spirit of the Great Crusade" at the end of the Malignax Campaign. 

A dour presence bereft of conviviality, the first captain excelled at reading the emotional and cognitive intent of his battle-brothers, commanding with a fluid and robust command style. Raldoron would also earn a reputation as one of the greatest warriors the Imperium had ever seen, compared in martial prowess to famed Astartes such as Ezekyle Abaddon of the Luna Wolves, Sigismund of the Imperial Fists and Jago Sevatarion of the Night Lords. 

Raldoron waged war in many joint campaigns with other Space Marine Legions, including the war against the Nephilim on Melchior alongside the Luna Wolves. It was upon the blasted soil of Melchior, amidst the ashes of the Nephilim's shattered dominion, that Raldoron bore witness to one of the earliest tragedies of the Red Thirst. 

The campaign, a joint undertaking of the Blood Angels and Luna Wolves, had ended in Imperial triumph, but the cost was not only measured in corpses and broken citadels. In the quiet aftermath, Raldoron found Brother Alotros -- once noble, now consumed, lost to the Red Thirst. Without hesitation, the first captain informed his liege-lord. Sanguinius, ever merciful, chose to face his fallen gene-son himself. When he emerged from that shadowed place, it was not alone -- Horus Lupercal was with him, and the truth could not be unmade. 

The secret of the Lost had passed to the Warmaster. Words were exchanged, sharp as blades. Azkaellon, captain of the Sanguinary Guard, gave voice to his fury. But Raldoron, ever composed, turned to pragmatism. He summoned Apothecary Meros of Captain Furio's 9th Company, ordering the extraction and destruction of Alotros' progenoid glands. "Speak of this to none," Raldoron commanded, and Meros obeyed. Thus was a secret sealed in fire and silence.

Council of Nikaea and the Battle of Murder

Later, Raldoron stood at Sanguinius' side as he journeyed to Nikaea, accompanied by Captain Thoros. There, beneath the Emperor's gaze, Raldoron was witness to the great Council of Nikaea that followed the Triumph at Ullanor. He sat among giants -- First Captain Typhon of the Death Guard, Constantin Valdor, Captain-General of the Legio Custodes, Fulgrim and Leman Russ, primarchs of the Emperor's Children and Space Wolves, respectively. He even lent his voice to the questioning of the outlander Kasper Ansbach Hawser. When the Edict of Nikaea was proclaimed, silencing the Librarius of the Space Marine Legions, Raldoron found himself at odds with Captain Nassir Amit, whose fury at the decree bordered on insubordination. "Take care with your words," he warned the captain of the Blood Angels' 5th Company, "or they will be your undoing."

On the killing fields of the world designated One-Forty-Twenty during the Great Crusade, known to some as "Murder", Raldoron once more followed his primarch, fighting alongside the Emperor's Children and Luna Wolves in bringing Imperial Compliance to that savage world. He later represented his Legion in the fateful Imperial delegation to the Interex, standing before Warmaster Horus to make the cause for war against those who had once welcomed them.

Signus Cluster Campaign

The ashes of the Kayvas Belt bore witness to another grim alliance -- this time with the Alpha Legion -- as Raldoron led his warriors in purging the Ork remnants of Ullanor. But victory was a mirage, and soon darker summons arrived. Chaplain Tanus Kreed and Captain Uan Harox of the Word Bearers brought with them a command from the Warmaster: the Blood Angels were to return to the Signus Cluster to finish what had been thought complete -- the eradication of the xenos known as the Nephilim. Horus, silver-tongued and secretive, claimed the xenos possessed knowledge that might quell the Red Thirst. The Blood Angels obeyed, stopping only long enough to collect a squad of Space Wolves commanded by Helik Redknife. 

The truth was betrayal. During the disastrous Signus Prime Campaign, the Blood Angels found that Signus Prime was no battlefield -- it was an altar to the Ruinous Powers. A trap, wrought by the hand of the Word Bearers' Chaplain Erebus and sanctified in ruin. There, Ka'Bandha, the great Blood Thirster of Khorne and Kyriss the Perverse, a Keeper of Secrets of Slaanesh, awaited, eager to claim the noble sons of Baal.

When the Blood Angels' flagship, the Red Tear fell from the skies and died after crashing into the Daemon-haunted world, Sanguinius called his captains to council. When Nassir Amit, his voice like fire, accused Horus of treachery, Helik Redknife revealed his unit's true purpose: they were a Watch Pack sent by the Wolf King Leman Russ himself. Should Sanguinius falter, the Space Wolves were to slay him. Even the Thousand Sons had defied the Edict of Nikaea, he claimed. Raldoron stood silent -- his heart shaken. But the Angel would not waver. He bade his gene-sons to cast aside doubt, and before the Council of Angels, Raldoron was among the first to swear the Oath of Blood against the Daemon Kyriss.

In the days that followed, intelligence gathered by the 1st Company revealed the emptiness of Signus Prime -- the populations of six cities, vanished without trace, their only remnant a single, monolithic temple: the Cathedral of the Mark. Sanguinius, burdened by foreknowledge and dread, confessed his fears -- that Horus, distant and cold, might have sent him here to die, to remove a rival. Raldoron dared not answer. In silence, he considered the unthinkable: civil war.

When Ka'Bandha struck and wounded the Great Angel, Raldoron rallied the Legion, retreating to the Red Tear's ruins. Madness took them. The Ragefire spread like a contagion. Amid the fury, Mkani Kano, once a Librarian, revealed a final hope: a Pariah named Tyllian Niobe, immune to psychic corruption. With Meros and Veteran Sergeant Orexis, Raldoron led a kill-team of the 1st Company to strike at the Daemon lords. Along the way, they found Helik Redknife and his Space Wolf warriors had been slaughtered by unknown Blood Angels.

In the sanctum of the Cathedral of the Mark, the truth of the Ragefire was laid bare: it was the soul of Captain Tagas, long thought lost on Murder, now tortured and made into fuel for Daemonic rage. Raldoron faced Harox and slew him, but Ka'Bandha would have claimed him too, had not Sanguinius returned, risen by the sacrifice of the Blood Angels' Librarians. When Kyriss offered to spare the Legion if Sanguinius took the curse into himself, it was Meros who intervened -- sacrificing body and soul to the black sacrament, holding the curse within himself. The Red Thirst was quelled, if only for a time. 

After the battle, Amit confessed to Raldoron that it was he who had slain the Space Wolves. But Azkaellon, ever the guardian of Sanguinius' image, had already lied -- to protect their primarch from shame, and to prevent division within the Legion. Unity, he claimed, was paramount. Raldoron, grim-faced, agreed to support the lie.

Horus Heresy

Raldoron followed his primarch to Macragge, becoming both commander and confidant during the forging of the Imperium Secundus. Through the horrors of the Ruinstorm, the fields of Pyrrhan, and the nightmare return to Davin, Raldoron was ever at his primarch's side. At the Battle of Beta-Garmon, he aided Sanguinius in propping up a faltering war effort, though the tide would not be turned.

At the Siege of Terra, Raldoron was entrusted with command of the Imperial Palace's Helios Gate. Beside Captain Maximus Thane of the Imperial Fists, he slew Gendor Skraivok, accursed commander of the Night Lords. At Colossi Gate, he fought shoulder-to-shoulder with the White Scars' primarch Jaghatai Khan, Captain-General Constantin Valdor of the Legio Custodes, and Marshal Aldana Agathe of the Imperial Army against Daemon and rot.

In the final assault of the Emperor aboard the Warmaster Horus' flagship, the Vengeful Spirit, Raldoron and his veterans stood beside their primarch. But as Sanguinius walked alone to his fate, the first captain and his warriors became trapped within the shifting region of the Warp known as the Inevitable City which overlapped with realspace on the Vengeful Spirit and even within the Imperial Palace. There, they felt the death of the Great Angel as a shattering psychic scream in their very blood. The Black Rage took them.

Raldoron, blade red with Loyalist and Traitor blood alike, fought beside his berserk brothers, his mind shattered, his soul lost. When the madness finally passed, he stood ashamed and confused, with only a dozen survivors. Together, they bore the broken bodies of the Emperor and their primarch back to the Imperial Palace. Raldoron led the mourning. With Azkaellon, Satel Aimery, and five hundred bloodied sons of Baal, he committed their primarch to legend.

When the Horus Heresy was over, the Legiones Astartes were sundered into smaller formations, known as Chapters, following the institution of the Codex Astartes during the Second Founding, and the IXth Legion became the Blood Angels Chapter. Raldoron assumed command as the post-Heresy Blood Angels' first Chapter Master. He bore Sanguinius' body back to Baal and placed it in a tomb of obsidian and fire, there to lie until the end of all things. 

He took part in the Great Scouring, hunting the Traitor Legions across the galaxy. As the first Chapter Master of the Blood Angels, Raldoron's blade would continue to sunder the enemies of the Imperium. He legend would live on, despite his ultimate fate being lost to history.

Personality

Raldoron was a study in contrasts: austere but perceptive, reserved yet unwavering in conviction. He did not possess the outward charisma of a warrior-king like Nassir Amit, nor the oratory brilliance of Captain Orfeo Cassandar of the Ultramarines. Indeed, Raldoron seldom spoke unless necessity or duty demanded it. Yet those who knew him well understood the depth beneath the silence -- a stillness born not of apathy, but of vigilance. It was this quiet gravity that drew the eye of Sanguinius, who saw in Raldoron not only a consummate tactician, but a custodian of the Blood Angels Legion's soul.

While not gregarious or prone to camaraderie in the manner of some of his brother-captains, Raldoron possessed a singular gift: the ability to read the burdens and tempers of his fellow Blood Angels with uncanny accuracy. He was a keen judge of character and motive, able to weigh the tensions between brothers and anticipate moments of doubt or weakness before they festered. His counsel to Sanguinius was always measured, drawn not from rash instinct, but from a deep and abiding comprehension of the mortal flaws and nobler aspirations that warred within every Astartes. In many ways, he served as the emotional anchor of the IXth Legion -- neither excusing their faults nor condemning them, but understanding them.

In battle, Raldoron was known to have favoured tried and tested weapons and tactics, those that brought the greatest chance for victory at the lowest cost to his Legion. Raldoron's style mirrored the angelic fury of his gene-sire, but it was tempered by a deliberate, almost surgical precision. His command philosophy was fluid, reactive, and often unorthodox. He held no attachment to rigid doctrines or grandiose strategies; instead, he adapted swiftly to the shifting tides of war, reshaping formations and exploiting enemy vulnerabilities with quiet brilliance. Though not as flamboyant in war as some of the IXth Legion's more storied captains, Raldoron's victories were often among the most decisive.

Despite his stoicism, Raldoron was no stranger to passion -- only that his was buried deep, where it could not cloud his judgment. He held fierce loyalties, not just to his primarch, but to the ideals Sanguinius represented: nobility, sacrifice, and restraint. These loyalties defined his worldview and, in turn, shaped his hatreds. Chief among them was his contempt for the Night Lords Legion. Long before the Horus Heresy tore brother from brother, Raldoron had regarded the VIIIth Legion as a stain upon the Emperor's dream -- agents of terror masquerading as soldiers, wielding fear and atrocity as tools of compliance. To Raldoron, their every act stood in opposition to the nobility of conquest. Their cruelty was not a weapon of necessity, but a reflection of the rot that festered in their gene-sire and his gene-sons. It was a hatred he made little effort to conceal, and one that hardened into loathing once the Night Lords declared for Horus.

Raldoron bore the burden of the Blood Angels' flaws with grim patience. He knew of the Red Thirst, intimately, and feared what it might make of them, but he did not shy from that knowledge. He carried it as he carried his blade: with solemnity, with duty, and with the silent, inward hope that their better nature might yet prevail. In the dying light of the Great Crusade and the dawning horrors of the Heresy, Raldoron stood as the quiet shadow beside the Angel, steady and enduring, even as the galaxy fell to madness.
