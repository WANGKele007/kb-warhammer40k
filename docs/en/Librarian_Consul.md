Librarian Consul was an officer rank of the Space Marine Legions used during the Great Crusade and Horus Heresy for an Astartes battle-psyker of a Legion's Librarius Corps. Librarian Consul was one of several specialty ranks that fell under the generalised banner of the mid-tier Legion officer rank of consul, formally known as a Legiones Consularis in High Gothic, which was awarded to an individual Astartes who was recognised for their unique talents and hard-won skills. 

For many years the Legions maintained cadres of Astartes battle-psykers in their ranks, warriors who fused these esoteric powers with a Space Marine's transhuman physical power. But many within the Imperium's hierarchy were troubled by such dangerous and unstable potential, and after dark events in the later days of the Great Crusade, such adepts were forbidden from the use of their arts and arcane lore by the Emperor Himself at the Council of Nikaea. 

These edicts were later violated and many Librarian Consuls began to use their abilities once more due to the Loyalists' need for psykers to defeat the malign powers of Chaos utilised by the Traitors during the Horus Heresy.

History

Librarius Project

The Imperial records concerning the origins of the Space Marine Librarius Corps are murky and mired in legend rather than hard facts like much of the history of the Imperium's early days. While Sanctioned Psykers had been used as part of the Great Crusade from its earliest days, their use had always been limited and never extended to the Legiones Astartes as a whole, or with any formality. Some emergence of psyker talent had been noted in several of the Legions, notably the Blood Angels and the Night Lords, although in numbers almost inconsequential to that found in the Thousand Sons. The Librarius Project sought to fuse together the manifest use of psychics in battle and the superior mental strength and physical prowess of the transhuman Space Marines, and regulate their function.

While Primarch Magnus the Red was not the project's originator, he became undoubtedly the project's chief architect and proponent, outwardly at least basing many of its tenets not on his own Prosperine culture but on other sources, perhaps less suspect to outsiders, and by this and his reasoned argument, Magnus persuaded a number of his brothers of the Librarius Project's virtues. The willingness and backing of several of the primarchs he swayed seems likely to have carried the day, and the project was sanctioned by the Emperor.

Members of several Legions who manifested psychic potential were then trained by the Thousand Sons in an adapted form of the Prosperine psychic disciplines and practices, fused with Terran- and Baalite-derived and Emperor-sanctioned lore. After completing this training, the Librarians were placed back into their Legions and trusted to further disseminate what they had learned to others of their brethren discovered to possess the abilities of a psyker. Though the greater mysteries of the Thousand Sons' arts were clearly withheld in retrospect, the newformed Librarians of the Legiones Astartes soon proved their worth. 

Librarians were ultimately the psychic specialists of the Space Marine Legions, channelling the fury of the Immaterium into bolts of lightning, gouts of searing flame, and all manner of spectacular supranormal techniques. Given the sheer power at their fingertips, these Warp-wielding warriors commonly rose to high ranks within their Legions. Few primarchs entirely shunned their skills until the Edicts of Nikaea were formally pronounced by the Emperor.

Council of Nikaea

As the 31st Millennium dawned, there were those within the Great Crusade who believed that the Thousand Sons Legion and their master had simply gone too far down a road long prohibited by the Emperor: the path of "sorcery." But what is sorcery? To many, particularly those of superstitious mind, the manifestation of any psychic power might be called such, and there is perhaps some truth to this belief as all psychic abilities, no matter how rigidly controlled or slight in effect, are perilous by their very nature. 

But the more common definition has always held that the term sorcery was more readily applied in the Age of the Imperium to the reckless pursuit of psychic power without due measures of control or caution, particularly the use of certain forbidden texts and practices of ancient Human and xenos origin and any practice that required bargaining for knowledge and power with the malevolent entities of the Warp. 

These arts, pursued in order to master forces of terrible power and breadth without any of the sanction or stricture the Emperor had enforced on His new Human civilisation, were to any sane mind understood to be incredibly dangerous, not only to the individual who sought them out, but to all around them. In short, they were the sins of the past wilfully repeated, and it was of this that the Thousand Sons were generally suspected.

The loudest voices that called for the censure of the Thousand Sons Legion for straying from the path of sanity and law were those of Primarchs Leman Russ of the Space Wolves, Corvus Corax of the Raven Guard and Mortarion of the Death Guard. All three had at various times come face-to-face with the all but unimaginable horrors that could result from the ultimate misuse of psychic power and feared that if left to their own devices, the Thousand Sons would bring such a calamity upon themselves and the worlds of the fledgling Imperium.

This opinion had behind it the weight of history, for the Old Night that had consumed the interstellar Human civilisation of the Age of Technology and brought about the long Age of Strife was often considered to have had its origins in the proliferation of powerful and unrestrained psykers. To their detractors, such as Russ and Mortarion, psykers in the ranks of the Legiones Astartes were fundamentally suspect. This was because they often had none of the overt limitations imposed upon the psykers of the Scholastica Psykana and little direct oversight as was found elsewhere in the Imperium. 

This was doubly the case with the Thousand Sons, a Space Marine Legion who by its gene-seed's influence at its core formed one of the greatest concentrations of psykers ever encountered, and as time progressed, suspicion grew of the outsider XVth Legion, its hidden ways and its arcane secrets.

Eventually, to address these growing concerns, in 001.M31 the Emperor called a great Imperial conclave, the Council of Nikaea, on the newly-terraformed planet of the same name, to determine whether Human psychic powers and the practice of psychic sorcery presented a danger to the newborn Imperium and its people. Ultimately, following the testimonials of the council, the Emperor refused any compromise and ordered all of the Space Marine Legions to disband their Librarius Corps and for all of the Librarians of the Legions to return to service as line warriors and never use their psychic abilities again.

Horus Heresy

Unfortunately, after the outbreak of the Horus Heresy, it soon became clear that the Traitor Legions and the other forces of Chaos could not be defeated unless the Loyalists were also able to draw upon the power of the Warp. 

Forced to break the Edicts of Nikaea, many of the Loyalist Legions were forced to reinstate their Librarius Corps in order to battle the malefic abilities of the Traitors as well as their summoned Daemonic allies. Every Loyalist Legion made use once more of their Librarians at some point in the galaxy-spanning civil war of the Horus Heresy. These warriors, still highly-trained experts in the use of the power of the Empyrean, proved invaluable during the conflict in the use of esoteric psychic powers that could annihilate foes in storms of ætheric lightning, or shield allies from devastating attacks.
