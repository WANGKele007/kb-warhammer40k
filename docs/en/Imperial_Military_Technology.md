Imperial Military Technology describes the various military technologies used by the Imperial armed forces in the Warhammer 40,000 universe. Sometimes considered to be covered by the term "wargear," which also includes weapons as well as the less lethal technologies described below, this equipment is grouped according to the appropriate category. 

With the evolution of the Warhammer 40,000 universe over some 40 years, many types of wargear have been described across the various editions of the setting. Some of these are no longer in use in the most current editions. Whenever appropriate, the history of the weapon is described, including some of the more notable units that are known to carry that weapon. Imperial armaments, weapons and vehicles have their own, separate pages.

The Imperium of Man is an immense, galaxy-spanning empire. There are several armed branches of the Imperium's military and police forces, and much of their equipment is common to multiple branches. The directly-employed armed forces of the Imperium include, but are not limited to, the Adeptus Astartes, the Astra Militarum, the Adeptus Mechanicus, the three Ordos Majoris of the Inquisition, the Adeptus Arbites and the Orders Militant of the Adepta Sororitas, better known as the Sisters of Battle.

Personal Armour

Mesh Armour

Mesh armour is an armoured, anti-ballistic bodysuit usually made from carbon-ceramic polyfibres. It can also be constructed in imitation of the much more advanced Aeldari mesh armour from tiny metal rings or thousands of thermoplas cells that are linked together. 

Phased out of use by the Astra Militarum in the 31st Millennium for Flak Armour, its use is ubiquitous elsewhere in the galaxy among paramilitary, pirate and other criminal groups; it always was far more common among the hive city gangs on the major Hive Worlds. The gangs preferred to use mesh armour for personal protection as all a ganger needed to make a suit was a powered nanoloom, water, and some powdered bone.

Flak Armour

Cheap and easy to produce, Flak Armour comprises several layers of ablative thermoplast materials and impact absorbent carbon fibres. Flak armour is usually designed as a vest or a sleeveless jacket which covers the upper torso. Useful for protection against shrapnel and low calibre weaponry, flak armour is the standard-issue personal armour of most Astra Militarum regiments.

Carapace Armour

Comprised of rigid plates made of armaplas or ceramite, Carapace Armour is made up of plates that are moulded to fit the wearer. Usually a suit of Carapace Armour will cover the torso and major joints with plates for the limbs along with a helmet, although it is also available in full body-suit form. Most wearers are in a relatively fit condition compared to other Flak Armour-wearing troops, due to the armour being so heavy. Carapace Armour can realistically be expected to stop a large variety of mid-range weaponry. 

During periods of relative peace, and during training exercises, some Space Marines will don Carapace Armour suits -- such garb befits a Space Marine during times of wait. Scout Marines are allowed access to Carapace Armour as they are not yet worthy enough to wear power armour and their Black Carapace has yet to have grown in so they are unable to properly interface with the armour. Carapace Armour is also used by the elite Tempestus Scion and Kasrkin squads and some regiments of the Astra Militarum from unusually wealthy or high-technology worlds.

Power Armour

Possibly the most prominent and best-known feature of the Adeptus Astartes is their power armour, a suit of ceramite plates with artificial myo-fibre bundles and mechanical servos that replicate the wearer's movements and enhances a Space Marine's already superhuman strength, as well as allows them to easily withstand environmental conditions and enemy fire that would kill a normal Human. 

The suit is directly linked to the nervous system of the Space Marines via the Astartes' Black Carapace implant. There are nine notable versions of Space Marine power armour to have been used by the Space Marines throughout Imperial history.

Mark I Thunder Power Armour - The first power armour created for the Space Marines. It was developed from the armour worn by the techno-barbarians that plagued Holy Terra near the end of the Age of Strife. It was used during the Emperor's campaign to retake and unite the Sol System before the Great Crusade. Power armour was by no means unique to the first Space Marines -- in fact, the techno-barbarian warriors the Emperor fought during the conquest of Terra wore it too, and the Emperor's earliest military forces, such as the Imperial Army and the Thunder Warriors, were wearing it even before the first Space Marines were developed. However, because it was designed with fighting on Terra in mind, it is not fully enclosed, with no means to support the wearer in a vacuum. Only the upper body is powered, due in part to the fact that during the Conquest of Terra, ranged weapons were difficult to make due to technological constraints, and so a warrior's upper-body strength was his most important asset. It takes its name from the Raptor Imperialis symbol the Emperor used before adopting the double-headed eagle after the signing of the Treaty of Mars.

Mark II Crusade Power Armour - This pattern of power armour was developed towards the end of the Age of Strife and was used during the dawn of the Imperium in the Emperor's Great Crusade to retake the lost worlds of Humanity. It was designed as a fully enclosed suit with life-sustaining systems that now allowed the Space Marines to fight in deep space and on alien worlds with hostile atmospheres or environments. The armour was made up of circular armour rings fitted under and over each other, making this armour relatively simple to repair. These precious relics are still used during ceremonial procedures by some Chapters of the Adeptus Astartes and given to champions as battle armour in others. These suits are also referred to as "Crusader Armour."

Mark III Iron Power Armour - With this pattern of power armour it was soon realised that fighting in the tight corridors of voidships was very dangerous. The Iron Pattern suit was designed to give greater protection to the Astartes. The frontal armour was much thicker (thereby giving greater protection), but the rear armour was thinner to compensate. The wedge-shaped helmet (designed to deflect incoming fire) formed the basis of the distinctive later Corvus Pattern beaked helmet.

Mark IV Maximus Power Armour - This pattern of power armour was to be the replacement for the old Mark II armour of the early Great Crusade; it was the first power armour pattern to incorporate many components used in modern marks of power armour. However, it was made from rare alien materials, and when the Horus Heresy broke out soon after it was issued, it was discovered that most Loyalist Space Marine Legions cut off from supplies behind the Ruinstorm could not find the materials needed to make it or repair it, and they often suffered irreparable damage during relatively minor battles.

Mark V Heresy Power Armour - This pattern of power armour was developed as a solution to the supply problems of the Horus Heresy. It was easily made from the most basic of materials, and it was easy to maintain. Almost all Space Marines wearing this pattern used spikes to hold the armour's ceramite layers together, which accounts for the pattern of studs shown universally on all examples of this armour. In time, it became traditional for the studs to be found on the head, lower legs, and left shoulder plate of this pattern of armour. Due to the sub-standard materials used in the construction of this armour variant, it had a tendency to over-heat quickly, and many Astartes had to endure sub-standard performance by turning down the power output of their backpack to prevent intolerable heat build-ups.

Mark VI Corvus Power Armour - This classic variant of power armour can be traced back to the time just before the outbreak of the Horus Heresy when its prototypes were tested by the Raven Guard Legion (hence the name) and this mark is still commonly used in contemporary Chapters of the 41st Millennium. It is most directly characterised by the beak-like muzzle of the Mark VI helmet and a studded auto-reactive shoulder plate on the left shoulder plate. The beak or muzzle led fans to nickname this variant "Beakie armour", and a dictionary of the Orkish language in the 2nd Edition Ork codex listed Marines as "beakies". This power armour represented the style of the first Space Marine models released by Games Workshop and did not get its Mark VI Corvus terminology until after models with the Mark VII styling were released. 

Mark VII Aquila Power Armour - Also known as "Imperator" armour, the Mark VII is the variant of power armour most commonly used by Firstborn Space Marines in the 41st Millennium. Characterized by the grim-looking vox-caster (external vocaliser) on the helmet's faceplate, and the plastron displaying the Imperialis (a winged skull), which has given the armour different names such as Armorum Imperator or "Eagle Armour" ("Aquila" being the High Gothic name of the two-headed eagle which symbolizes the Imperium). A variant of the Aquila pattern's helmet is known to exist, yet is no different from the regular helmet, save for an armoured trim added around the external vocaliser.

Mark VIII Errant Power Armour - The Mark VIII Errant pattern is an update to the Mark VII Aquila Power Armour, and is distinguished by a raised collar at the front to deflect incoming projectiles away from the vulnerable helmet joint and armoured plating for the torso power cables, which became standard after Techmarines had already moved in most Chapters to modify their suits of Mark VII armour to fix this vulnerability. Full versions of this armour are almost never seen in the 41st Millennium because of the small number that were manufactured; however, it is common to find parts of it being used by Astartes officers and Veterans on the battlefield.

Mark X Power Armour - This modular suit of power armour was designed by Archmagos Dominus Belisarius Cawl for the Primaris Space Marines during the Era Indomitus. Unlike previous patterns of Space Marine armour, it came in a variety of different types intended for use in different tactical situations.

One design feature of Marks VI through VIII of power armour is their adaptability; it is fairly common within most Space Marine Chapters to see a suit combining parts originally created for three or four suits, salvaged from dead battle-brothers on the battlefield. The designs of Mark VII through Mark X armour were created with this simple modularity in mind, though the Mark X took the concept to its fullest extent.

The Sisters of Battle and several high-ranking members of the Inquisition also use power armour, but they are not directly linked to their suits in the way Space Marines are, which decreases the armour's effectiveness.

Artificer Armour

Space Marine Techmarines may choose to craft a suit of power armour of extraordinary quality; such suits are referred to as Artificer Armour. Artificer Armour is crafted from the finest materials and with the utmost care. Due to this attention to detail Artificer Armour provides more protection than normal power armor but does not suffer the compromise of lessened mobility, like Terminator Armour. 

Artificer Armour is often highly decorated and embellished with ornamentation and devotional scripts. Most Techmarines wear Artificer Armour of their own crafting; it's construction is a final test of their acquired skills following their tutelage under the Adeptus Mechanicus on Mars. 

Artificer Armour is kept in the Chapter Armoury as a reward for devoted service by a Veteran Astartes, or as standard equipment for specialised offices in the Chapter. 

Terminator Armour

See Tactical Dreadnought Armour
Tactical Dreadnought ('Terminator') Armour was developed for close-quarters fighting, where staying power was more important than maneuverability. The technology was originally intended to enable maintenance crews of voidships to operate in hard vacuum or in other adverse environmental conditions. It was combined with power armour technology and brought about the invention of Terminator Armour during the Great Crusade. It was hoped that it would replace power armour, but was relegated to its current role due to a lack of maneuverability.

Terminator Armour is manufactured from a ceramite-plasteel alloy as an exoskeleton with servo-assisted interfaces with the user's own neurological and muscular systems to enhance movement. It is the heaviest armour in any Imperial arsenal, is able to withstand tremendous punishment, and serves as a solid heavy-weapons platform in open-field combat. 

The elite 1st Company of most Space Marine Chapters uses Terminator Armour extensively, and only those Veteran Astartes who earn the Crux Terminatus are permitted to wear it. It is also said that a fragment of the Emperor's own armour that He wore during the final battle with Horus at the Siege of Terra is built into the Crux Terminatus but if this is true, in order to put a piece of the relic into each suit of Terminator Armour ever made, only the tiniest slivers of the sacred artefact were put within. 

Less frequently, high-ranking members of the Inquisition have been known to wear Terminator Armour. Notably, members of the Ordo Malleus can be seen wearing such suits.

Individual Wargear

Auspex

An auspex is a general term for hand-held scanning devices used by squad leaders and officers in all Imperial fighting forces to survey friendly and enemy positions and to gain information on surrounding terrain and buildings.

Generally they have a series of antennae and a small liquid crystal display screen, though some appear to have the ability to link with orbital sensory systems on space stations and Navis Imperialis starships equipped with more powerful sensors of the same type called augurs.

Bionics

Bionics are cybernetic replacements for injured or missing organic body parts and organs. These can vary from subtle augmentations designed to replace damaged limbs or organs to radical alterations of the Human body. Tech-priests of the Adeptus Mechanicus often replace at least one of their organic limbs with bionic ones or augment their bodies with various entirely new cybernetic appendages like servo-arms or Mechadendrites.

Cameleoline

Cameleoline is a special fabric often used in camouflage attire by special forces of the Imperium. It changes colour to their surroundings, helping the wearer to hide.

Frenzon

Frenzon is a collective term for a cocktail of psychoactive drugs injected into a recipient (willing or unwilling) in order to manipulate their psychological state. They are only used by a few Imperial units, such as the Penal Legions. Once administered, the subject becomes fanatical and fearless for up to ten solar minutes, which can be a lifetime in battle.

Jump Pack

Jump Packs are bulky, vectored thrust engines that can be worn by a single soldier, designed to be attached to the Space Marine's, Sisters of Battle's and Chaos Space Marine's existing powered backpack. They are used to make powered jumps across the battlefield, allowing the troopers wearing them to quickly engage their foes. Jump Packs are thus used by assault troops, primarily the Assault Marines of the Space Marine Chapters, and the Seraphim of the Sisters of Battle. Jump Pack-equipped infantry are often dropped from low-flying Thunderhawk Gunships and deep strike into combat, as the Jump Pack also functions as a makeshift anti-gravity parachute.

Medi-pack

Varying from containing a simple first aid kit to a full diagnostic computer and a suite of sophisticated drugs and synthetic flesh, the medi-pack generally helps a medic to heal wounded or ill troopers in a battlezone. Sometimes in particularly vicious and dangerous warzones, medics will have to scrounge for medical supplies and beg for the necessary drugs from aid stations and passing Munitorum cargo transports.

The narthecium is a medi-kit/medi-pack used by Space Marine Apothecaries and are fully automated units. They carry drugs and equipment that are geared to repairing the hardier constitution of a transhuman Space Marine, and thus can be dangerous to use on normal Humans. A narthecium also includes a reductor, an instrument which allows the Apothecary to withdraw the precious gene-seed from a fallen Space Marine's Progenoid Glands so that the Astartes' gene-seed returns to his Chapter to create new initiates.

Adepta Sororitas Hospitallers have a scaled-down version of the Space Marine narthecium, usable on normal Human anatomy -- and, in the service of the Inquisition, for torture. Many an Imperial servant has had reason to thank the gentle touch of the Hospitaller, and many an enemy of the Emperor has had bad experiences with this "gentleness."

Servo-arm

A servo-arm is a special, cybernetic tool used by the tech-priests of the Adeptus Mechanicus to assist them in repairing mechanical equipment. They can also be used to great effect in close combat, being roughly as effective as a Power Fist.

Shields

Refractor Field

The refractor field is a common sight amongst Imperial Guard regimental officers and other less elite Imperial organisations. The refractor field confers reasonable protection against most attacks, though less than styles of conversion fields. When issued to Imperial Guard officers the circuitry for a refractor field is often enclosed in a gorget mounted below the officer's neckline.

Rosarius

The Rosarius is a powerful conversion field generator issued to Space Marine Chaplains and members of the Ecclesiarchy and Inquisition in the form of a religious symbol of the Imperial Cult. A conversion field by its very nature provides much greater protection than a refractor field, and is thusly a much rarer sight. 

The most common users of a Rosiarus are Space Marine Chaplains, who are gifted one on a pendant (usually a skull-cross or Imperial Aquila) by the Eccleisiarchy to show the bond between the latter organisation and the Adeptus Astartes. Senior priests and Inquisitors can also sometimes be seen wearing one of these pendants.

As an aside, it is notable that the Iron Fathers of the Iron Hands Space Marines -- who embody a combined function of Chaplains and Techmarines -- are gifted a Rosiarus not by the Eccleisiarchy but by the Adeptus Mechanicus, showing a stronger bond between the technologically-minded Marines with the latter organisation over the former.

Iron Halo

The Iron Halo is a Space Marine relic of which only one generally exists in a Chapter at any one time. It will usually be given to one of the senior captains or other officers as a reward for exceptional service. 

Some Chapters, like the Dark Angels, issue one to each of their captains. The Iron Halo incorporates a powerful conversion field similar to that used in a Rosarius (it provides the same level of protection) and is often seen mounted on top of a Space Marine's backpack.

Storm Shield

The Storm Shield is a hand-held shield used by both Space Marines and members of the Inquisition and their Chambers Militant, most commonly seen carried by Terminator Assault Squads in conjunction with the Thunder Hammer. It contains a conversion field of lower strength and smaller size than those used in a Rosiarus or Iron Halo, only providing sufficient area and energy to absorb close-range attacks in melee. A Storm Shield is carried like a normal medium or heavy shield, tying up the bearer's hand and forgoing the use of a second hand weapon.

Combat Shield

The Combat Shield is a cut-down, buckler-style version of the Storm Shield, but it is unclear whether it can deploy a conversion or refractor field. It is used exclusively by the Space Marines, and like the Storm Shield is only useful in melee situations. 

Unlike the Storm Shield however, the Combat Shield does not tie up the user's hand (being strapped to the forearm) and allows the use of a second hand weapon or firearm.

Targeter

A targeter is a device issued to some elite Imperial Guardsmen and Space Marines. It usually appears as a scope of some sort attached to a firearm intended to greatly increase the user's ranged accuracy.

Teleport homer

The Teleport Homer produces a signal which can be locked onto by teleporting troops, thereby increasing their chances of arriving on-target, as well as the added benefit of increasing their chances of surviving.

Teleporter

An ancient technology dating back well before the Horus Heresy to the Dark Age of Technology, teleporters are still used by the Imperium, but are considered rare and valuable devices. They are not fully trusted by even the most experienced Imperial commanders, as a malfunction could be fatal since users are briefly transported through the non-Euclidean space of the Immaterium to reach their destination without crossing through the intervening distance in realspace. 

Teleporters are used mostly by the Space Marines to assist during certain types of assaults. Usually the devices are only present on battleships, as the transport of something even the as small as a Human body takes immense power. Use of teleporters is almost exclusively the province of Terminators, with a few exceptions given to specialised Chapters such as the Grey Knights. 

One notable instance of teleportation gone horribly wrong which showcases the dangers of this technology was during the Siege of Haddrake Tor, when the Ruinous Powers of Chaos used sabotage to disrupt the teleport homers of the besieging Imperial Fists' Terminators and caused many of the 1st Company to be entombed in solid rock, including the captain of the 1st Company, Kleitus.

Vox-caster

A vox-caster is an Imperial communications array, similar to a standard radio, that can be as small as helmet-mounted commlinks to massive banks of equipment at an Imperial headquarters. Usually a squad or platoon will have a link to their unit headquarters via one vox unit and/or micro-beads for trooper-to-trooper communications. 

Command units usually use more complex versions of vox casters -- known as "master voxes." These can receive and transmit on multiple frequencies at once, making them ideal for coordinating many squads or companies simultaneously. In absence of any vox communications, troops are taught to sign to each other when in battle.

Space Marines and Sisters of Battle (and high-ranking members of the Inquisition and Adeptus Mechanicus) have miniature vox-casters built into their power armour (or cranium in the case of the Adeptus Mechanicus). Most of these are short-ranged devices and used only for squad-to-squad communication.

The Imperial Infantryman's Uplifting Primer

The Imperial Infantryman's Uplifting Primer is a small book issued to all guardsmen in the Astra Militarum that is full of Imperial Creed prayers and descriptions of basic tasks an infantryman must perform. Regulations and forms are also part of the book to ensure each trooper has their own instruction manual to life in the Imperial Guard. 

Troopers are encouraged to read and memorise most of the primer, while disgruntled regiments may refer to these books as "toilet-paper" (and occasionally use them as such). The Primer was published by the Departmento Munitorum, yet some sections appear to be subtle satire against the Imperium itself from a loyal, yet disgruntled guardsman, for example the sections on "Duty to Commissars".

Vehicle Wargear

Dozer blade

The Dozer Blade is, as its name suggests, a bulldozer shield attached to the front of an armoured vehicle to allow it to move through rough terrain easier. In Astra Militarum armies, they are referred to as "rough terrain modification" and can take other forms, such as chainsaws attached to hydraulic arms.

Extra Armour Plating

Some Imperial vehicles have additional armour plates bolted or welded onto their hulls. These are collectively referred to as Extra Armour Plating, and minimise the damage and shockwaves suffered by the vehicle and its crew whenever it is hit by kinetic projectiles. 

Improved comms

Found in Imperial Guard armies, improved comms is an upgrade to a vehicle representing improved communications equipment, such as that found in battlefield command vehicles.

Pintle mount

A pintle mount is an optional addition to an Imperial vehicle that allows an anti-personnel weapon to be attached to an Imperial vehicle's exterior. This weapon is used, either by the vehicle's commander/veteran guardsman or by remote control, to engage secondary targets and immediate threats while the gunner focuses the vehicle's main firepower against enemy armour or other major objectives.

Storm Bolters are the most common weapon used with a pintle mount, although Heavy Stubbers are also used by the Astra Militarum. The Black Templars deviate significantly from this usage, mounting Multi-Meltas to their Land Raider Crusaders. The Traitor Legions and Heretic Astartes warbands also use pintle-mounted weapons, usually a Combi-bolter, although Combi-flamers and Combi-meltas are also used.

Searchlight

Searchlights are attachments found on various vehicles of the Imperium to aid reconnaissance and search missions.

Smoke Launchers

Smoke Launchers are attachments found on various vehicles of the Imperium.

Void Shields

Void Shields are Warp-based energy field defensive devices usually employed by warships and Titans of Imperial manufacture (some vehicles now under the power of Chaos still maintain these devices). The Void Shield is a powerful defence system that can stop all but the most powerful weapons. 

A Void Shield appears as a shimmering curtain possibly akin to looking at something through a thin layer of water. The circuitry in Void Shield generator systems creates a strong smell of ozone prior to being activated. Any object coming into contact with the shield once activated is not damaged but makes a sizzling sound. 

Any objects, unless fired at or striking the shield with phenomenal power simply bounce off or are halted in mid-air. Organic materials, liquids or simply energy with harmful potential (e.g. a laser beam) are stopped or dissipated off harmlessly into the Immaterium. Explosive munitions will detonate on contact. 

Any object caught in the field when activated is simply sliced in half. For instance, a Human body caught in an activated Void Shield will be sliced in half as if viewed in cross-section and munitions caught in this way are destroyed explosively. This makes the Void Shield deadly, if impractical in offensive rather than defensive situations.

Void Shields are usually layered, meaning that a Titan or starship isn't protected by just a single void shield but by several layers of individual shields. The front shield will absorb the incoming fire until it overloads and collapses, at which point the second layer shield becomes the front shield until it collapses, at which point the third shield becomes the front shield, and so on. 

Meanwhile the tech-priests aboard the Titan or voidship would repair and restart the downed shields and so create a new layer. Theoretically, this means that Titans and voidships are invulnerable unless the enemy can sustain heavy enough firepower to collapse all void shields faster than they can be regenerated.

Chaos Space Marines

It should also be noted that much of the weaponry and wargear used by the Chaos Space Marines also come from this list -- especially the equipment wielded by the Loyalist Adeptus Astartes. Since the Horus Heresy, the Imperium has made some advances in technology; thus much of the Heretic Astartes' wargear is of older design, or the Chaos Marines simply have restricted or no access to some of the items listed above.

For example, the Chaos Marines possess Plasma Weapons -- however, their Plasma Cannons, being much older, are simply too large to be carried by a single man, even one with the transhuman strength of a Traitor Marine; thus they are always vehicle-mounted. Rather than Storm Bolters, which were first designed around the time of the Heresy, the Chaos Marines possess older Combi-bolters -- which are essentially two normal bolters combined into a single gweapon, but the effect is not quite as effective as the purpose-built Storm Bolter. This has, however, given Chaos pintle-mounted Combi-bolters a special utility unknown to Imperial vehicles: the weapon can be adapted to consist of a single bolter combined with a single-shot flamer or meltagun.

Further still, the Chaos Space Marines have extremely limited access to Jump Packs, and no access to Assault Cannons, Psycannons or the special bolt ammunition types for most boltguns (except inferno bolts), as all of these were invented after the Heresy. Furthermore, the Psycannon which was developed as an anti-Daemonic weapon would be unsuitable for the use of the Traitor Legions and other Chaos Space Marine warbands as it is anathema to the forces of the Warp they serve.

However, some wargear is replaced by similar Daemonic gear. For example, some Heretic Astartes can be seen with mutated wings, which act similar to Jump Packs. Chaos Space Marines can have their power armour mutated by the powers of Chaos, creating Chaos Power Armour, which can be similar to Artificer Armour in the protection it provides depending on the "blessings" of the Dark Gods the Heretic Astartes has received. Lastly, the Iron Warriors Legion is known for salvaging Imperial siege equipment, such as the Space Marine Vindicator.
