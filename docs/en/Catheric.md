The Catheric faith, known as Catherism, was an ancient religion of Old Earth still practiced in some form during the early 31st Millennium at the time of the Horus Heresy. Those who still practiced the religion during the early days of the Imperium of Man were looked down upon by others because of their refusal to accept the atheistic doctrines of the Imperial Truth.

Catherics believed in a personal god and the cross-like symbol known as the "crux" served as the symbol of the faith, and was often worn around the neck by the faithful. The faith's traditions included the use of smoke-filled censers which were carried in a procession in some forms of the faith's primary worship service.

Among the holidays held dear by the Catheric faithful were the recurring feasts of Cathermas, Radmastide and the celebration of the Divine Architect, along with the observances held in its builder lodges. The Catheric faith was known for its attempts to enforce a strict form of morality and had been formed by the unification of several prior Human religious proto-creeds. All of these faiths were known for their well-developed eschatology, beliefs in a divinely-prophesied apocalypse that would one day strike Terra but in which Humanity would be saved by the appearance of a morally righteous saviour. Of course, the appearance of this saviour would also lead to the birth of a new and more just society for all Humankind, wiping away all the suffering and injustices of the past.

A known pious Catheric of this period was Ollanius Persson, a former Imperial Army trooper and Perpetual who had retired from his service in the Great Crusade to the world of Calth in the Realm of Ultramar in the hope that being on the Imperial frontier would make it easier to practice his ancient religion.

It is unknown if the Catheric faith survived in some form after the founding of the Imperial Creed as the state church of the Imperium in the 32nd Millennium.

History

Despite Catherism's deep belief in an apocalypse that would finally lead to the fulfillment of its faithful's millenarian hopes, when the Age of Strife finally struck the galaxy in the 25th Millennium and Terra was ravaged by the complete breakdown of Human civilisation, the promised divine saviour did not emerge on Terra. Catherism's faithul had no explanation for why the saviour had not come when the apocalypse seemingly had, but some among their theologians theorised that the sins of Humanity had proven so great in their god's eyes that no salvation was truly possible until they had atoned and earned their opportunity at redemption. However, once the Unification Wars began in the 29th Millennium and conditions on the homeworld began to improve, many among the Catharists started to view the Emperor as the promised saviour.

As the Emperor rose to power over Terra during the Unification Wars, He in time was viewed as a living god instead of a purely Human saviour, despite His repeated efforts to deny any form of divinity. The worship of the Emperor became so widespread by the start of the Great Crusade that the practice of other surviving Human religions like Catharism began to decrease dramatically, as their gods and doctrines had not proven their worth to the faithful. The growing cult of Emperor-worship seemed far more fruitful for the religiously-inclined who rejected the atheism of the Imperial Truth. 

Surprisingly, Catherism escaped extinction and in time, many of its remaining worshipers began to build a new city on Terra where the Catheric faithful could find a home and a haven. It was to be called "Ur", and the city was meant to serve not only as the embodied hope of the faith, but also as a base from which the faithful could determine Mankind's moral status in the eyes of their deity in the wake of the Age of Strife. Ur was never completed, for over one hundred and forty standard years after the start of the Great Crusade, a force of raiders attacked the Catheric work camps and communes that surrounded the rising edifice of Ur, killing the labourers who were tasked with completing the construction of the city. 

The religion was still being practiced some twenty standard years after the assault on the construction site, though it is not known how open Catheric worshipers could still be about their adherence to the faith at that time. Kasper Ansbach Hawser, the Human scholar who eventually became a skjald or oral historian of the Space Wolves Legion, and the Daemon who long appeared to him in the guise of his friend the Conservator (archeologist) Navid Murza, were both known to be Catherists, but they each approached their worship differently. Hawser was from one of the communities that had been destroyed while it was building Ur and did not seek to hide the fact that he was raised in the Catheric faith. While he no longer believed in the Catherist doctrines, Hawser still had respect for the strict morality it preached. Murza, meanwhile, hid his Catheric beliefs and secretly wore a crux, but he was publicly scornful and mocking of anyone who was openly religious in defiance of the Imperial Truth. 

The Perpetual Ollanius Persson was also a faithful Catheric who left Terra and later settled on the Ultima Segmentum world of Calth in the Realm of Ultramar once Ultramar was brought into the Imperium. Persson openly practiced his faith and wore the crux, but he had realised early on that in the face of the Imperium's aggressive attempts to spread the Imperial Truth and wipe out all religions, he would be able to live more freely far out in the frontier of the Ultima Segmentum and away from the Throneworld.

Trivia

The Catheric faith is based in part on the doctrines, practices and symbols of the real world Roman Catholic Church.
